    Scaling this nationwide

    The Baltimore version has three parts that change at national scale: where the studio list comes from, when the check runs, and how studio websites stay fresh.

    A national studio list

    OpenStreetMap already covers the whole country for free, but small studios are patchy in it. Foursquare's open places dataset fills most of the gap: over 100 million businesses, free for commercial use and refreshed monthly. Daxko's own Salesforce accounts and closed-lost deals add the studios sales already knows. Records are merged on website domain and phone number.

    Run on new leads, not on demand

    A HubSpot workflow fires when a contact arrives with a personal-mailbox domain or no associated company. It calls the checker and writes back the company association, contact status and tech stack. The same logic could live in a Clay table if the team would rather own it there. Cost grows with inbound volume, not with the number of studios in the country.

    Read studio sites ahead of time

    Every studio site is re-read on a monthly schedule and stored, so checking a lead becomes a fast lookup instead of a live crawl. Sites that go dead or get squatted fall into a recovery queue that searches for the studio's new site. At volume, a paid search API replaces the free search used here.

    The crawl doubles as a market map

    Recording every studio's booking software shows competitor share metro by metro: where Mindbody is entrenched, where studios still book through Wix or Square, and which ones already run a Daxko product. That's a targeting list for displacement and greenfield campaigns that doesn't depend on anyone filling out a form.

    People stay in the loop where it matters

    Only verified owners route automatically. Everything else goes to a review queue with the evidence attached, and each SDR confirmation becomes a labeled example for tuning the matching rules.
